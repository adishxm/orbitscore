# Research source register and use rules

## Source precedence

For a claim about current regulatory obligations, use the currently effective official text from the relevant regulator/agency or standards publisher first, with exact section, jurisdiction, scope, and effective date. Check amendments, notices, and applicability; a PDF filename or team summary is not enough. For agency technical tools, use the official user guide/release notes. For scientific claims, prefer the original peer-reviewed study or authoritative technical report and inspect methods, assumptions, and date. For user or market claims, direct interviews/observations and transparent datasets are stronger than desk-research conjecture. Vendor pages are useful for product-feature comparison but are not independent proof. Team dossiers and project papers are secondary interpretations, not primary authority.

If an official source is missing from the library, stale, inaccessible, or ambiguous, research it again before using it to change a rule, make a public factual claim, or advise a real mission. Record date accessed. Keep differing sources and interpretations visible.

## How to record a source-backed claim

For each material claim, record:

- Claim in the project’s own words.
- Source title, issuing author/body, stable URL or local path, publication/version/date, and date accessed.
- Exact page/section/table/paragraph or API endpoint.
- Source type: statute/regulation/order, standard/guideline, agency manual/report, peer-reviewed work, dataset/API docs, vendor material, interview, or internal analysis.
- Applicability and jurisdiction; conditions, exclusions, units, and assumptions.
- Exact source statement versus the team’s interpretation.
- Conflicting or outdated sources and remaining confidence/limitations.
- Code, fixture, requirement, user-facing label, or decision affected, plus a test or review method.

Do not cite a document generally when an exact clause/page is available. Do not present an internal profile as an official rule. Never let a secondary summary substitute for inspecting the primary source when the claim affects a real-world decision.

## Known limitations of this archive

The copied archive is preserved from an earlier project bundle. Its original `README.md` and `RESEARCH_CATALOG_AND_INDEX.md` are retained as supplied; their descriptions are not independent verification of every file, source URL, characterization, or claim. Verify authenticity, integrity, version, scope, and access date before relying on an item. Some items are HTML snapshots or documentation copies that can become stale. The archive does not itself supply permission to redistribute beyond the applicable terms of each source; do not republish source documents publicly without checking rights.

The event pages are time-sensitive and should not be used as current status without fresh official confirmation. User-uploaded files and internal dossiers are useful for project history, but they do not become independent sources simply because they are included here.

## Source types must not be conflated

A regulation/order, a voluntary standard, an agency guideline, a software manual, an academic model, an API description, a vendor product page, and an internal interpretation have different authority and purposes. Preserve those labels in the product and research notes. “Official” describes who issued a source, not whether it applies to a given mission or proves a product implementation correct.
